About the practice

Most private matters start as a question you can’t yet settle.

Often it’s a question you can’t answer with the information you already have, and you’d like a clearer view before you decide anything.

Before you get in touch

Start with the questions that matter.

It helps to know the point of the conversation, whether that’s a fact you hope to place or a decision you aren’t ready to make yet. If you can answer these for yourself, your first message will mostly write itself.

  1. 01

    What are you trying to understand?

    Put it in one or two plain sentences.

  2. 02

    What do you already know?

    Only the outline. The detail can wait.

  3. 03

    What is still missing?

    The gap you can’t fill on your own.

  4. 04

    Roughly when did it happen?

    A general timeframe is enough, if you know it.

  5. 05

    What should wait until you’ve spoken?

    Documents, photographs and other people’s private details.

These are prompts for your own first message. They are not a list of services.

Your first message

A clear first note.

Whether you call or write, these four points are enough to start.

Say enough for a reply, and no more.

Hold back for now

  • Account numbers and identity documents
  • Photographs of people
  • Other people’s addresses or private numbers
  • Long attachments and a full narrative

Please do not include sensitive case details in your initial email. If the conversation continues, ask then how anything further should be shared.
